Utah Building Code (IBC Chapter 15) Tear-Off vs. Layover Analysis

Under active Utah commercial building codes (IBC Chapter 15), properties with a single existing membrane are permitted a certified commercial layover if non-destructive moisture verification confirms the insulation and decking are dry and structurally sound.

  • Certified Layover Protocol: Mechanically fastened or adhered over high-density DensDeck® cover boards, saving property owners tens of thousands in landfill fees while retaining 100% eligibility for 30-year GAF NDL warranties.
  • Full Tear-Off Protocol: Mandatory when two or more roofing layers exist or when infrared moisture scans reveal water saturation within existing insulation cores.

Commercial Roofing Technical FAQs

What is a GAF No Dollar Limit (NDL) Warranty?

An NDL Warranty is a premium commercial guarantee where GAF covers 100% of material and labor costs to repair any manufacturing or certified installation defect for up to 30 years with zero financial cap on payouts.

Can we install a TPO or PVC membrane over our existing commercial roof in Utah?

Under Utah building codes (IBC Chapter 15), a single-layer flat roof in dry, sound condition can receive a certified layover. RHIVE conducts core sampling and thermal infrared scans to verify structural qualification, saving tens of thousands in tear-off costs.

What is the primary operational difference between TPO and PVC membranes?

TPO delivers superior thermal reflection and UV resistance for standard office and warehouse spaces. PVC contains plasticizers making it impervious to animal fats, kitchen grease exhaust, and industrial chemicals, essential for restaurants and processing plants.

How does RHIVE eliminate salesman markups on commercial projects?

We operate using AI-driven aerial diagnostic workflows and salaried technical project managers, eliminating the 10-15% commission paid to sales reps and returning those savings via transparent itemized pricing showing materials, labor, overhead (<10%), and profit.
